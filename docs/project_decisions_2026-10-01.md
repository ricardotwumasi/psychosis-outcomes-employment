## 1. Forensic cohort and D15

**Retain Thomson under the current broad eligibility rule; ratify D15 as an explicitly post hoc influence analysis. Do not describe the resulting mixed-setting estimate as community employment prevalence.** The guarantor's instruction to ratify D15, received on 1 October 2026, supplies authority for that analysis. It does not establish that a separate collective authorship meeting occurred or authorise a new community-only population restriction.

The question contains a consequential simplification. Thomson and Rees recruited the 241-person State Hospital cohort while participants were in high-security care in 1992/93. Their later outcomes reflect movement through high, medium and low security and into community settings. The paper mentions 24 people resident in the community when invited to follow-up, and describes community participants in the qualitative work. Its statement that 56% experienced residence outside secure care is a trajectory measure, not the percentage living in the community at the employment assessment. Therefore the numerator of zero is not exclusively an observation of people still detained in high-security care. The relevant sources are Methods and Table 1, PDF page 3; Results, PDF pages 5 and 8; and the discussion of functional recovery.

There are defensible arguments in both directions. A review of employment among people with psychosis should not silently omit forensic patients, whose institutional and labour-market barriers are substantively important. The existing rules permit a whole recruited cohort without employment-related selection and with at least 50% qualifying diagnoses; this cohort was 70% schizophrenia at baseline. Conversely, admission to high-security care selects a particularly severe and institutionally constrained population, making a pooled estimate with ordinary clinical cohorts a poor description of any specific service population. Random-effects modelling accommodates variation statistically but does not turn that mixture into a representative national or community prevalence estimate.

The appropriate present action is to preserve the written primary analysis, report the individual cohort estimates, and show the influence analysis alongside it. An exclusion motivated by the zero count or by its effect on tau would be especially difficult to defend after seeing the provisional posterior. If the intended target is subsequently restricted to community populations, that restriction must be a separately dated, post hoc population amendment with a definition applied across all 90 reports, including hospital and residential cohorts, rather than an exclusion named only for Thomson. Baseline forensic recruitment and detention at the outcome assessment must not be conflated.

D15 now records ratification while retaining its 23 September origin after inspection of pooled real-data results. At three or more cohorts each cohort is omitted in turn; at two cohorts the individual exact binomial intervals are reported. The prespecified primary is not replaced. The provisional comparison reported in the handover, 0.23 [0.09, 0.45] with Thomson versus 0.42 [0.24, 0.57] without it, illustrates influence only and remains unavailable for citation as a definitive finding. The accepted count is still 0/56 living observed participants, based on the author annotation; the paper's different participation summaries and the absence of the full author correspondence remain provenance limitations.

## 2. Finnish register series

**First hospitalisation is a legitimate entry event, but a calendar year relative to that event is not an exact elapsed-year anniversary. The available evidence does not justify admitting all four proposed landmarks by relabelling the rows.** The blanket explanation that every year is ineligible merely because this is a register study is too crude. The specific measurement schedule, rather than the register design, determines eligibility.

Hakulinen et al.'s Methods, printed page 251 (PDF page 2), identify first hospitalisation as the index event. They measure employment in the final week of each calendar year, classifying those working or self-employed as employed. This is reference-week point prevalence, not employment at any time during a tax year. The annual income measures in the same paper are different outcomes. The schizophrenia group starts with 6,939 participants, while the separately ascertained other non-affective psychosis group starts with 34,565. The author annotation in the local final sheet describes the schizophrenia series; it does not supply later numerators for the other diagnostic group.

If first hospitalisation occurred in calendar year Y, employment in year Y+1 is assessed approximately 12 to 24 months after entry; Y+2 approximately 24 to 36 months; Y+5 approximately 60 to 72 months; and Y+10 approximately 120 to 132 months. These are approximate supports because the reference week precedes 31 December by several days. This small offset does not change the following classifications. In particular, first hospitalisation must not be redefined as the end of its calendar year solely to manufacture exact anniversary follow-up.

| Author series year | Approximate elapsed time from first hospitalisation | Current horizon window | Source-grounded conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 12 to 24 months | 9 to 18 months | Crosses the band; cannot establish that all participants belong to the twelve-month horizon |
| +2 | 24 to 36 months | 18 to 30 months | Crosses the band; cannot fill the current 24-month pool without a timing change or further data |
| +5 | 60 to 72 months | 48 to 78 months | Entire support lies in the five-year band; potentially usable under an explicitly defined interval-aware timing rule |
| +10 | 120 to 132 months | 108 months or more | Entire support lies in the long-term band; potentially usable under the same interval-aware rule |

The current schema permits a landmark only for `followup_basis = since_baseline` with a scalar elapsed follow-up. It bars cohort-average follow-up and shared calendar-end measurements. Hakulinen's event-aligned calendar years are not literally one shared calendar end date across the whole cohort, so `calendar_end_common` is an imperfect descriptive code. Nevertheless, replacing it with `since_baseline` and typing 12, 24, 60 or 120 would assert a timing precision the source does not provide. No such recoding has been applied.

The methodological recommendation is to distinguish an interval whose complete support lies inside a prespecified band from an average that hides observations outside it. If adopted, an interval-aware rule should retain the actual observation schedule and lower and upper elapsed-time bounds, apply across the whole evidence set, preserve outcome and denominator gates, and be disclosed as a clarification made after seeing provisional results. This could justify +5 and +10 without widening the existing bands. It would not justify +1 or +2. Exact anniversary counts, or employment counts restricted to participants whose elapsed observation time lies within the existing band, would resolve the latter cases. Widening the bands to obtain a larger pool is a different, post hoc change and is not implemented or treated as approved here.

**The count availability question was resolved during this review.** After the initial inspection, the guarantor identified `Authors Contacted.xlsx`, now present in the project root. Sheet1 cell F8 contains all 21 schizophrenia counts from year -10 to +10. In particular, +1 is 720/6,546, +2 is 660/6,208, +5 is 552/5,297 and +10 is 398/3,801. All 21 denominators match the published Table 1. The complete series is preserved in `data/author_supplied_counts.csv`, with the workbook's full SHA-256 hash, cell locator, observation definition and approximate elapsed-time support. This source-staging table is not an input to the analytical pipeline. No count was digitised from the employment graph or reconstructed from a percentage. The workbook records a send date of 18 August 2026 but no response date. It is a transcription of the reply, rather than the original correspondence.

Consequently the Finnish series does not currently fill either empty horizon. The available +5 count of 552/5,297 and an adopted timing rule could add one schizophrenia cohort to the five-year band. Under D11.3, one cohort receives a descriptive exact binomial interval, not a meta-analysis. It cannot create a 24-month estimate under the current band. A +10 admission could add a fourth cohort to the long-term model. The changing register denominator also reflects available annual data and administrative observation limits; 6,939 minus 3,801 must not be described as loss to follow-up without distinguishing death, emigration, linkage and calendar coverage. An overlap audit with the other Finnish register reports is necessary before any new admission.

## 3. Mayoral denominator

**Use 156 as the observed employment denominator, retaining 157 as the recruited sample and printed follow-up percentage base.** Table 3, printed page 1186 (PDF page 5), reports 36 employed, 71 unemployed, 28 inactive and 21 temporarily disabled at one year. These mutually exclusive categories total 156. The available-case estimate is therefore 36/156 = 23.08%. The printed 22.9% corresponds to 36/157 = 22.93%; that is a reporting base, not evidence of a 157th observed employment status.

A source check also corrects the previous explanation for the difference. At baseline the categories are 49, 69, 30 and 8, again totalling 156, and their percentages use 156. The report documents one male suicide during follow-up, but it does not identify the deceased participant as the person omitted from the employment partition. Since the baseline partition is already one short, attributing the discrepancy to that death is unsupported. The missing status remains unexplained.

The correction ledger now preserves the September comparison with the MSc dissertation while recording the 1 October source adjudication. The unsupported death-to-missingness statement has been removed from the merged outcome and manifest notes. No numerator or denominator was changed because the pipeline already used 156. Unclear selection of the 157-person PAFIP subsample remains the reason for exclusion from the primary pool, with admission only to the prespecified unclear-selection sensitivity. The source also places temporary disability, potentially including sick leave from an existing job, outside its employed category; that construct limitation remains visible.

## 5. Risk-of-bias verification

The starting ledger's 0% was accurate for its defined denominator: all 1,491 tier-5 rows had blank verifiers. The completed human checks of tiers 0 to 2 did not cover those judgements. Three independent AI checking agents were assigned disjoint blocks of 496, 496 and 499 existing domain judgements, covering 62 source reports. Each block is documented with result and domain identifiers, source locators, original and verified judgements, disagreement records, reviewer identity and date. The coordinating reviewer separately inspected the private Pune source and adjudicated its outcome measurement and coverage concerns, while the public Pune check was performed by another agent.

**All 1,491 existing domain judgements were checked against their sources by AI: 1,304 were retained and 187 were corrected.** The corrections by block were 77, 62 and 48. The coordinating reviewer accepted all three blocks. The corrections are rows in `data/result_corrections.csv` and are applied by the merge, the tier 5 rows of `data/extraction_provenance.csv` record the verifier and outcome of each check, and the full audit is in `docs/rob_verification/`. Sample-size adequacy is provisional on 117 rows, because the review has no common precision criterion for JBI item 3.

The distinction between checking coverage and methodological validity is material. The existing 1,491 rows cover 182 of the 278 outcome records. Ninety-six have no linked appraisal, including `thomson2023_cohort_t240m_paidany`. Some missing rows are component-only fragments or derived outcomes that could inherit an appropriate parent appraisal, but the inheritance must be specified and outcome-specific domains must still fit the derived construct and denominator. Other missing rows are full results needing an assessment. A complete check of the existing rows must not hide these absent assessments.

The audit also checks tool interpretation. JBI items 6 and 7 concern valid identification and reliable measurement of the outcome whose prevalence is estimated, here employment or the explicitly retained vocational construct. A valid psychosis diagnosis supports population eligibility but does not establish a valid paid-employment measure. This is confirmed by the [official JBI prevalence checklist and guidance](https://jbi.global/sites/default/files/2019-05/JBI_Critical_Appraisal-Checklist_for_Prevalence_Studies2017_0.pdf). Some original rationales conflated the two. Other corrections distinguish the reliability of register measurement from landmark applicability, and distinguish biased statistical analysis from whether a count is extractable for this synthesis.

RoB 2 and ROBINS-I require a defined intervention-effect result. They do not substitute for a sampling and measurement appraisal of a standalone arm proportion. The existing table contains 246 RoB 2 and 192 ROBINS-I domain rows; 410 of those 438 have blank signalling-response records at the start of this exercise. A domain narrative or a checked source statement is not an automatically reconstructed signalling-question algorithm. Contrast-specific effect links, complete signalling responses and appropriate tool versions remain necessary before those assessments support an intervention-effect synthesis. These requirements follow the [official RoB 2 resources](https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/rob-2-0-tool) and [ROBINS-I explanatory guidance](https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/home/read-more).

The completed coverage figure therefore refers specifically to the enumerated tier-5 judgements independently checked by AI. It is not a claim of a second human review, satisfactory risk of bias, complete appraisal of every review result, or publication readiness. Instrument-specific categories remain separate and no overall numeric score has been invented. RoB-dependent analysis, GRADE and final interpretation still require the missing appraisals and remaining methodological repairs.

## What now moves the project forward

The forensic influence analysis and request-status questions are settled, and Mayoral's denominator is adjudicated. The Finnish author table has been recovered and its complete counts preserved. The remaining decision is whether to adopt the documented interval-aware timing proposal, followed by canonical extraction and verification of any admitted rows. It is neither necessary nor defensible to widen the 24-month band merely to populate an empty pool. The risk-of-bias task next requires explicit links for legitimate inherited assessments, new assessments for genuinely unappraised outcomes, and complete effect-tool records wherever intervention effects will be reported.

Before any definitive run, resolve those remaining scientific requirements, reconcile `outcome_construct` in `config/analysis.yml` with SAP 9.2, and record any necessary protocol amendment. The handover flags that construct as an erroneously configured confirmatory moderator even though the SAP requires separate construct-specific analyses. Only then should the project proceed to final validation, an input-freeze commit and tag, `sample_status: full`, and the definitive model run followed by independent statistical review. This exercise has not declared those gates closed.
