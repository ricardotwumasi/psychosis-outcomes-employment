## Why the denominator falls, and what can be said about it

The paper never explains the fall. It states one rule (no data in a given year, not included in that year's analysis; p. 251), shows the resulting counts in Table 1 (p. 252), and says nothing about attrition, death, emigration or censoring in its Limitations (p. 254). Four mechanisms are compatible with the Methods and cannot be separated from the paper. (1) Calendar coverage: cases were first hospitalised between 1988 and 2015 and FLEED ends in 2015 (p. 251), so a person hospitalised after 2010 cannot have a year +5 record and one hospitalised after 2005 cannot have a year +10 record. This must account for a large part of the fall, but the paper gives no distribution of first hospitalisations by calendar year, so its size is not known. As an illustration only, if entries were spread evenly over the 28 years, calendar truncation alone would remove about 1,240 people by year +5 and about 2,480 by year +10, against observed falls of 1,642 and 3,138; the even-spread assumption is not supported by anything in the paper. (2) Death and (3) emigration: FLEED and FOLK are population registers of residents, so people who die or leave Finland would cease to have data, but the paper does not say so for cases and reports no deaths or emigrations (the only related statement concerns controls, who "had to be alive and live in Finland during the year when the case participant was diagnosed", p. 251). (4) Register scope: FLEED records "the entire Finnish working-age population" (p. 251) and cases could be up to 60 at diagnosis, so some may age out of the register within ten years; the age bounds of FLEED are not stated. One feature of Table 1 is worth recording: the schizophrenia control count is exactly five times the case count in every one of the 21 years (for example 26,485 = 5 x 5,297 and 19,005 = 5 x 3,801). That is what purely set-level causes (calendar window, birth-year-matched age limits) would produce, and it is also what deletion of whole matched sets would produce; individual deaths or emigrations handled person by person would break the ratio. The paper's wording ("the corresponding case or control participant") does not settle which applies. Consequently 6,939 minus 5,297 (or 3,801) must not be described as loss to follow-up; it also cannot be described as purely administrative. If death removes people from the denominator, the later proportions describe survivors still resident and registered, and the direction of any resulting bias relative to all entrants is not determinable from the paper. The later-year observations also over-represent earlier entry cohorts (first hospitalised 1988 to 2010, or 1988 to 2005), which is a matter of applicability across calendar periods and not of measurement.
