# Independent AI risk-of-bias source verification, assignment 2

Date: 1 October 2026. Verifier: Codex AI independent verification agent 2.

All 496 assigned existing domain rows across 21 reports were examined against their specific result, timepoint, outcome and source evidence. There are 434 judgement agreements and 62 judgement disagreements. The accompanying CSV contains one record per original assessment identifier and retains original judgements. Corrections have not been applied to frozen shards or merged extraction tables by this agent.

The local complete page-labelled PDF texts were consulted with focused reading of recruitment, eligibility, methods, outcome definitions, relevant tables, attrition and limitations. Repeated domain rationales were checked once at their shared source level and revisited for every distinct outcome, timepoint, denominator and arm. Jäckel Figure 1 was rendered and inspected because its boxes were not present in the extracted text. Clarke's cross-report sampling and attrition claims were checked against Killackey2019; Gjerdalen's flow and death claims against Evensen2017. Availability of the full text is not evidence of access to missing supplementary appendices or author datasets.

JBI condition identification and measurement must concern the prevalence outcome being assessed. Psychiatric diagnostic ascertainment instead establishes population eligibility. Several original judgements were numerically defensible but had rationales about schizophrenia diagnosis in an employment domain; those rationales have been replaced. This interpretation follows the [official JBI prevalence checklist](https://jbi.global/sites/default/files/2019-05/JBI_Critical_Appraisal-Checklist_for_Prevalence_Studies2017_0.pdf), and aligns with the outcome-based interpretation already used in other shards. Clear constructs are assessed as their own outcomes. Non-separability of paid work from education does not automatically invalidate a clearly defined work/study composite. Conversely, Ringbom's EET-labelled NEET complement includes parental leave and remains an outcome-identification concern.

Instrument routing still requires repair: Harrow, Jäckel and Mervis store arm-specific counts with comparative tools. These rows are not themselves treatment-effect estimates. Conditional appraisal of the source comparison cannot establish prevalence applicability. RoB 2/ROBINS-I signalling responses and a properly specified comparative estimand/target trial remain absent. Human verification is not inferred from this AI audit, and coverage of existing rows does not establish coverage of missing assessments, instrument validity, GRADE readiness or publication clearance.

The [official DRKS00018107 record](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00018107), accessed on 1 October 2026, establishes retrospective registration on 21 January 2021 and recruitment starting 23 August 2017. It leaves sequence generation and allocation concealment unreported, lists no blinding and provides no protocol document. The article instead reports blinded raters. Retrospective registration cannot establish allocation concealment, and missing separate employment counts cannot prove outcome-dependent selective reporting of the reported EET comparison. Selective-reporting high has therefore been revised to some concerns, with EET-wide overall ratings reconsidered. Education-subgroup high ratings remain due to explicit post-randomisation selection and unexplained denominators.

The local Authors Contacted.xlsx workbook, Sheet1 cell F8, supplies the Finnish schizophrenia series for years -10 through +10. All 21 denominators match the published Table 1 exactly, and the year-0 numerator 968 matches the article. The other 20 numerators are author-supplied evidence, not independently printed article counts. The supplied year-0 percentage 13.9% has a small rounding inconsistency because 968/6939 is 13.95014%, conventionally 14.0% at one decimal place. The counts should be preserved and proportions calculated from them. Sheet1 cell F7 confirms a Moncrieff reply without numerical outcome data. These checks do not extend this assignment beyond its existing 496 RoB rows.

**andersen2024.** PDF pp. 3-6 and 8-9: service-consent cohort of 1574 FEP participants; 751 observed statuses at 9 months and 578 at 12 months; collection ceases at treatment termination. Table 2 and the NEET operational definition checked independently. Separate FEP and UHR panels are essential.

**baltazar2022.** PDF pp. 2-3 and 6-8: all first admissions in a defined catchment; 142 baseline patients, 108 survivors, but only 43 observed occupational statuses at T4 (6 regular-market jobs, 8 occupational therapy, 29 no occupation). Insurance-based extrapolation outside CAPS is not the extracted employment measure.

**blackman2025.** PDF pp. 2-3 and 5: 124 recontacted NIMH research volunteers, with source recruitment denominator unreported; 56 employed at follow-up. Paid work is explicitly defined; the last-year window is given in the limitations. Four retirees and one indeterminate status are excluded. Baseline-to-follow-up interval is not the outcome recall period.

**chang2016.** PDF pp. 2-3: 515 eligible FES/FEMP patients, 420 completing three years, with subgroup sizes 374 and 46. Record collection protocol, inter-rater reliability and attrition comparison checked. Sustained-employment definitions differ between Methods (including study) and table footnotes (work only). Printed FES employment percentage cannot reproduce on 374.

**clarke2023.** PDF pp. 3-5 and 7: secondary analysis of the vocational-interest-selected EPPIC trial, 145 baseline participants in this report and 100 at 18 months; 37 currently in paid work. Parent Killackey 2019 PDF pp. 2-3 confirms 171 assessed, 25 excluded, 146 randomised and 18-month response associated with studying at 12 months. Missing appendices remain unavailable.

**falk2016.** PDF pp. 2-4 and 8: complete in/outpatient register enumeration of 756 first diagnoses (421 men,335 women); national income linkage. Employment is defined through registered paid income. Tables report age-standardised rates and confidence intervals, rather than crude counts. That extraction limitation is distinct from statistical bias.

**gjerdalen2023.** PDF pp. 3-7: JUMP referrals/self-referrals selected for vocational rehabilitation, 148 participants,33 competitively employed at five years. Mandatory employment/tax registers measure at least one month in the last calendar year. Parent Evensen 2017 PDF pp. 4-5 confirms 319 referrals,61 decliners,25 not starting rehabilitation, and 2 deaths before two years.

**hakulinen2020.** PDF pp. 2-5: national first qualifying inpatient diagnoses, with 6939 schizophrenia and 34565 other nonaffective psychosis participants at year 0; employment 968 and 11335 respectively. FLEED employment is work/self-employment in the last week of each calendar year. Table 1 shows shrinking denominators beyond year 0. Hospital diagnosis validity is not employment measurement validity.

**harrow2017.** PDF pp. 2-5: consecutive young acute admissions; 58 of 70 schizophrenia participants at 20 years; 9/36 currently prescribed antipsychotics versus 16/22 not prescribed. Work scale includes half-time students and carers. Current medication exposure is selected and confounded; joint comparative appraisal does not make these arm-specific rows whole-cohort prevalences.

**hui2026.** PDF pp. 2-4 and 7-9: 360 consecutively recruited trial participants,120 standard-care participants excluded from this analysis,234 with baseline income and 219 at four years. Assessments and employment binary checked; Table 3 reports 40 and 21 unemployed using across-group percentages. Actual employment item denominator and paid-work definition are not stated.

**jackel2025.** PDF pp. 2-4 and 6-7: 110 randomised,94 reaching baseline,90 analysed (46 IPS,44 TAU). Figure 1 was also rendered and visually checked. Upstream eligible 119 is a flow typo/inconsistency; downstream counts reconcile. Primary EET36/46 versus 24/44; any EET38/46 versus 26/44. Education subgroup 25/29 is selected after allocation. Official DRKS00018107 checked on 2026-10-01.

**liangrong2021.** PDF pp. 2-4 and 6: 276 childhood-onset first admissions,170 with regular follow-up,106 excluded for several reasons including changed diagnosis; 89 at school,70 employed,11 neither. Follow-up 0. 2-16. 8 years, median 5. 6. Two clinicians extract records and two specialists check them. Variable endpoint timing is not itself proof of nonstandard ascertainment.

**lindgren2020.** PDF pp. 2-4: Helsinki catchment recruitment with consent-capacity gate; 67 with baseline cognition,54 with one-year clinical data,53 with occupational status. Self-report and case records ascertain work/study without sick leave at the one-year interview; 26/53 reproduces 49. 1%. Parent recruited denominator remains unreported.

**mervis2017.** PDF pp. 2-5: 64 vocationally selected clinically stable referrals, randomised 29 IVIP and 35 support group; supported-employment tally 14/29 and 6/35 at 12 months. Unreported allocation method and unclear tally source/construct remain. Degrees-of-freedom discrepancies for cognitive/other scales do not establish missing supported-employment data.

**moncrieff2025.** PDF pp. 2-4: RADAR trial 253 randomised,190 follow-up assessments. EET counts 8/66 and 30/124 in Table 2 conflict with employment-completion fractions in the text and with Table 4 counts 57/132 (total 189). Whole-cohort derived 38/190 remains dependent on this unresolved discrepancy. Employment item/source is not specified.

**nossel2018.** PDF pp. 2-5: OnTrackNY real-world programme 325 entrants with at least one quarterly follow-up; 260 with six-month outcome in the text,124 with any paid employment and 81 competitive. Definitions explicitly separate school, paid employment and minimum-wage competitive jobs. Staggered enrolment, censoring and discharge partly explain changing bases 262/261/260.

**peebo2022.** PDF pp. 3-7: 199 initial FEP department participants,107 attending all reassessments; Table 2 a completer outcomes and Table 2 b changing denominators 166/154/107. Recruitment text does not explicitly state consecutive or complete sampling. Employment is undefined; follow-up percentages have no numerator and some do not reproduce on table headers. Register outcomes at 10 years are different measures.

**petrakis2019.** PDF pp. 4-10: IPS caseload requires desire for competitive work and capacity for 8 hours/week; 137 referred-and-engaged,136 linked. 63 achieve a broad vocational outcome,31 competitive,8 noncompetitive,24 unspecified, and 17 education. Table 4 includes volunteer/unpaid placements. Missing employment-type records affect competitive ascertainment. Education has its own certificate/diploma/university categories.

**ringbom2023.** PDF pp. 2-4 and 6-7: whole 1987 Finnish birth cohort; 55171 retained and 288 psychosis cases, with 86 schizophrenia/schizoaffective cases in Table 1. Salaries, pension contributions, student benefits, jobseeker programmes and parental-leave benefits have explicit register definitions. Table 1 concerns 2008-2015 cumulative periods, not point prevalence at age 28. Later text gives 137 schizophrenia/schizoaffective cases without resolving the 86 discrepancy.

**topor2019.** PDF pp. 2-5: 447 first-diagnosis catchment participants,364 working-age follow-up members; only 53 institutionally recovered and 159 continuously-institutional-contact subgroup salary percentages reported. Groups depend on last five years of service contact; 152 intermediate members omitted. Table 3 reports 60% and 27% salary at year 10 with no cell counts/denominators.

**xu2020.** PDF pp. 2-5 and 8: consecutive 1000 record block yields 118 hospitalised under 18 schizophrenia cases. 65 interviews, including 59 parent informants,48 untraceable and 5 refusals; 3 deaths leave 62 survivors. 34 unemployed,23 employed,5 school; 16 economically self-sufficient. Outcomes rely largely on unvalidated proxy information; surviving 62 is explicitly stated on p. 3.

The principal source-grounded changes include correcting outcome-domain diagnostic rationales; Falk statistical-analysis no to yes; Gjerdalen measurement unclear to yes; Blackman measurement no to unclear and response-rate no to unclear; Hui outcome measurement yes to unclear; Liangrong measurement no to unclear; Lindgren identification unclear to yes for its stated composite; Mervis missing outcome data some concerns to low for the complete tally; Peebo sampling yes to unclear and completer-frame yes to no; outcome-specific Petrakis education measurement; Ringbom subgroup sample adequacy no to unclear; and Topor subgroup frame yes to no. Xu explicitly reports 62 survivors after three deaths, so its statistical-analysis rationale no longer claims silent exclusion of the deceased.

No review-specific prevalence precision threshold is available, so retained sample-size judgements remain qualitative rather than algorithmically validated. Register response-rate categories vary between yes and not applicable across source shards; those acceptable instrument categories do not erase coverage or censoring concerns. Missing appendices, contested denominators and unresolved constructs remain visible rather than being treated as cleared because an assessment row was read.
